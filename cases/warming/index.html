What the data shows

The long view

The ten-year average rose from −0.21°C in 1880–1889 to +1.01°C in 2016–2025, relative to the same 1951–1980 baseline.

Year-to-year variation

The annual anomaly fell from +1.28°C in 2024 to +1.19°C in 2025. Both years sit far above the baseline.

Check the months

All twelve months of 2025 were above +1°C relative to the corresponding month in the baseline period.

Conclusion

The sustained rise is visible across decades and across months. The cooler result in 2025 compared with the record year of 2024 sits within that long-term warming trend.

Sources and methodology

NASA GISS Land–Ocean Temperature Index (GISTEMP v4). Annual anomalies use NASA’s published January–December column. Each trailing mean uses the current year and the nine preceding complete years.

This is the global surface temperature anomaly, not a local forecast or a reading of absolute temperature. NASA describes uncertainties in the historical reconstruction. The snapshot ends in 2025 to exclude incomplete years.
