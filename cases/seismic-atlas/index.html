What the data shows

A concentrated geography

The epicenter map shows repeated clusters around the Pacific margin. Many events in the catalog are offshore.

A steep drop at higher magnitudes

Of the 3,874 catalog earthquakes of magnitude 6.0 or above, 387 were magnitude 7.0 or above, about one in ten.

Depth varies

The catalog recorded 3,063 shallow events below 70 km in this selection, about 79% of all events. The two largest catalog events in the window both had magnitude 9.1.

Conclusion

Large recorded earthquakes cluster geographically, and magnitude 7.0 or above events are a small fraction of the magnitude 6.0 or above catalog. This distribution provides historical context for exposure. Local hazard also depends on distance, depth, soil, buildings, and tsunami exposure.

Sources and methodology

USGS FDSN Event API, UTC dates from January 1, 2000 to January 1, 2026 exclusive, event type earthquake, and preferred magnitude 6.0 or greater. Depth uses the preferred catalog estimate. Natural Earth provides the map geometry.

Catalog records are revised and depend on reporting. A point shows an epicenter rather than the geographic extent of shaking. Annual counts and epicenter locations do not predict where or when earthquakes will occur.
